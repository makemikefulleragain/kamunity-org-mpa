Ring Zero · Sovereign Infrastructure · Not in primary nav — you found it

"Your community has been paying rent to landlords you've never met. Every message, every file, every relationship — sitting in infrastructure owned by someone who can change the price, the algorithm, or the terms. You already know this. That's why you're here."

Ring Zero is a proposed path towards community-owned tools and infrastructure. This page explains the design, not a ready-to-purchase instance or guaranteed delivery plan.

What the approach could include

These are design aims, not a confirmed package. We would agree on a practical scope before any work begins.

1
A forked instance that's yours

A possible model is an independently controlled codebase, database and domain. Ownership, support and exit arrangements would need to be tested and agreed for the actual implementation.

2
The constitutional floors — built in, not bolted on

The proposed constitutional principles include privacy, data control, clear exit terms, shared learning and human-scale coordination. Whether each principle is technically enforced must be checked in a real instance; this page is not a security certification.

3
Capability transfer, not dependency creation

The aim is capability transfer rather than lasting dependence. A possible sequence is Constitution → Fork → Configure → Test → Launch → Handover; actual timing and support would depend on the community and the build.

4
Federation without merger

Future instances might exchange selected public signals and resources without pooling private member data. Federation and any trust badge remain design goals, not currently verified capabilities.

The journey

An illustrative six-stage sequence, not a six-week commitment. Timing would be agreed after scope and readiness are understood.

1
Stage 1
Constitution

Read the founding documents. Understand the floors. Decide if this is right for your community. Nothing technical yet.

2
Stage 2
Fork

Fork the codebase. Set up your Supabase. Your instance, your credentials, your data from day one.

3
Stage 3
Configure

Configure selected tools and public information for your community. Kai remains a proposed capability, not part of a live offer here.

4
Stage 4
Capability Transfer

You learn to build doors. Add tools. Manage signals. We hand over the keys. You test that they work.

5
Stage 5
Launch

If acceptance checks pass, consider a soft launch with agreed support and a rollback plan.

6
Stage 6
Independence

Confirm the community can operate what was delivered and knows how to get help or leave.

Whether this approach fits should be decided before committing to a build. Confirm licence and handover terms for any specific implementation.

Who this is for — and who it isn't

This isn't a rejection. It's information. Knowing it's not for you yet is useful.

This is for you if —
  • Community organisations with 5–150 active members — the Dunbar range where this works
  • Orgs that hold sensitive community data and care who owns it
  • Orgs tired of paying rent to platforms that don't share their values
  • Orgs with at least one person willing to learn to maintain it — it's not hard, but someone needs to care
This is not for you if —
  • You're looking for a plug-and-play SaaS — this requires ownership, not subscription
  • You need an immediately available, fully managed service — this approach requires scoping and testing
  • You're a large institution looking to scale extraction with better branding — the constitutional floors will reject this organically

Doug runs a community land care group in the Wheatbelt. Twelve volunteers. They coordinate via a Facebook group they don't control and a Google Drive folder they'd lose access to if their Gmail account got hacked. Doug has never heard of data sovereignty. He just knows that every time Facebook changes something, they lose members.

Does Ring Zero serve Doug's community?

Yes. But not yet. Doug's community needs the tools first — the free ones, the practical ones, the ones that don't require any sovereignty consciousness to use. Ring Zero comes later, when Doug's community is ready to own the infrastructure they've already been using.
